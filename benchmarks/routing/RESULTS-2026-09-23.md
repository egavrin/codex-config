# Routing benchmark, 2026-09-23

## Decision

Keep the current default route. GPT-6 Luna High Standard → GPT-6 Sol Medium
Standard scored higher than GPT-5.6 Terra Medium Fast → GPT-6 Sol Medium
Standard under the configured-tier credit estimate, but it did not pass the
predeclared promotion gates. Both routes missed a critical token-logging finding
in all three repeats of `review-typescript-session`. The score ordering also
reverses under the permitted extremes of unobserved service tiers.

This decision does not change the active `~/.codex` profile or the repository's
historical configuration snapshot.
The active global config inspected at reporting time named GPT-6 Sol High as
its root, while this experiment's baseline was the pinned Terra route. The
experiment therefore decides whether the Luna route merits promotion over
that pinned baseline; it does not score the installed Sol-root configuration.

## Results

Each route ran three fresh repeats of each of 24 scored tasks. The tasks are
equally weighted across six classes. Three earlier small pilot tasks and two
historical paired tasks remain calibration material and contribute no score.
Their preserved seed files or prompts are listed in `calibration.json`.

The paired task bootstrap gives a Luna-minus-Terra score difference of **14.21**
points, with a 95% interval of **10.06 to 17.55**. The acceptance difference is
**−1.39 percentage points**, with a 95% interval of **−4.17 to 0.00** points.
The planned quality non-inferiority threshold of −5 points is met. Under the
most unfavorable allowable tier assignment, however, the scores are **82.18
Luna versus 87.32 Terra**. The favorable assignment gives **87.62 Luna versus
71.78 Terra**. The observed rollout JSONL has model, effort, parentage and
token counts but no reliable effective tier for any of the 144 runs. Credits
above are therefore estimates, not observed subscription charges.

## Data quality and interpretation

- All 144 planned runs are present; all have a root, token usage, an external
  acceptance result, and no malformed rollout lines. All 96 delegated runs
  have the expected GPT-6 Sol child. The root sessions show the requested
  GPT-5.6 Terra Medium or GPT-6 Luna High, and children show GPT-6 Sol Medium.
- The 24 baseline seeds each fail their target external check and pass their
  environment and baseline checks. The agent workspace did not contain the
  external `checks.json` file. Saved workspaces were independently rechecked
  after the runs.
- The review rubric was amended after an initial false rejection of valid
  database-error wording. Review tasks were rerun with the amended rubric.
  Two further valid paraphrases, one CLI and one fetch review, were accepted in
  the final checker. It was applied uniformly to all 144 saved workspaces;
  exactly two review grades changed from fail to pass. Original local records
  are preserved. This post-run grading change is a methodological limitation.
- The global Codex config hash changed between the non-review and review cycles.
  The pinned experiment profiles, runner, agent guidance, price snapshot and
  Codex version matched. The runner passed explicit root/child model, effort
  and tier overrides, but hashes alone cannot reconstruct other inherited
  global settings. This is an additional comparison limitation.
- The fixtures are synthetic and relatively small. These results do not yet
  estimate the frequency or complexity of tasks in the user's actual workload.

The dated [Codex credit rate card](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing) and
[speed documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/speed)
support the Standard rates and the 2.5× Fast multiplier used here. That
multiplier applies to GPT-6 Astra, Sol and Luna where Fast is available, as
well as GPT-5.6 Terra. The calculation charges the root and every child,
including failed attempts and follow-up turns.

## Reproduce and extend

The source fixtures, prompts, seed commits, external checker, profile files,
rate snapshot and runner live in this directory. Run `runner.py self-check`
before a new cycle, then the `run`, `analyze` and `verify` actions as described
in [README.md](README.md). The public
[aggregate summary](results/2026-09-23-summary.json) contains the score,
class, task, uncertainty and completeness breakdowns. Local raw rollouts,
agent workspaces and run records remain under ignored `artifacts/` because
they can contain machine paths and environment data.

The next comparison should pin and archive the effective runtime config,
obtain actual per-agent tier or billing telemetry, and use an independently
frozen review checker before launching runs. Add larger public cross-component
and real-workload-like tasks, especially security reviews, before reconsidering
the default route.
